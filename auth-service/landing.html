Your course, mapped like a night sky

StudyNova turns your own slides, PDFs, web pages and lectures into a map of concepts. It quizzes you from that material, shows the source for every claim, and when you get something wrong, points to the earlier idea you are most likely missing.

Sign in to your workspaceWatch the 2-minute demo

Built at HackUMass XIV for students who want answers they can check.

From scattered notes to a plan in four steps

Everything StudyNova shows you comes from the material you give it.

  1. Collect

    Upload PDFs and slides, paste a link, add a YouTube lecture, or save any page with the Chrome extension. Each source lands in your course, exam and module.

  2. Map

    Build the concept web. StudyNova finds the key ideas and how they depend on each other, and quotes the page that supports every link.

  3. Practise

    Take a quiz or a boss battle written from your own pages. Every question cites where its answer comes from.

  4. Fix the gap

    Get a question wrong and StudyNova walks back to the weakest earlier concept, then builds a revision plan around it.

What you can do

Built so you can trust what it tells you, and see where every answer came from.

A concept map with sources

See every idea in your module as a star, coloured by how ready you are. Click any link to read the two quotes, one from each concept's page, that justify it.

Concept web, material map and keyboard outline

Find the likely gap

When you miss a question, StudyNova follows the prerequisite links back to the weakest concept and lights up the path. It says "likely gap", never "the cause", because it is an educated guess you can check.

Shown with the evidence page

Quizzes from your pages

Multiple choice, short answer and past-paper style questions at three difficulty levels. A quote that is not really on the cited page is dropped, never shown.

Boss battle

Three hearts, XP, and a boss whose health is the module's concept count. Missed concepts come back as the boss's next attack until you clear the module gold.

Revision plan

Tell it how many minutes you have a day. It orders concepts by exam weight and how weak you are, so the most useful work comes first.

Lecture captions you can fix

Lecture audio is transcribed using your slides' own vocabulary. Words that were probably misheard are highlighted, and one tap fixes them. Fix a word twice and it is fixed automatically next time.

Ask your materials

Ask a question in plain words and get an answer with citations. If your material does not cover it, StudyNova says "not in your materials" instead of guessing.

Chrome extension

Save the page you are reading to the right module in two clicks. The popup remembers where you saved last, and uses your existing sign-in rather than asking for a password.

Comfortable to read, by design

Dark navy instead of black and warm off-white text instead of pure white reduce glare, which many readers with dyslexia find easier. The letterforms use Atkinson Hyperlegible where it is available.

How to use StudyNova

A short guide to every part of the app. Open any step.

1Sign in and set up your course
  1. Choose Sign in at the top right and enter your team's username and password.
  2. In the sidebar, select + next to Courses and create a course (for example, Neural Networks).
  3. Add an exam with its date, then a module inside it (for example, Backpropagation). The exam date drives your revision plan.
2Add your material
  • Upload a PDF or PowerPoint with Add material. Tag it as lecture, notes, past paper or model paper. Past papers decide which concepts weigh most.
  • Paste a link to a web page, or a public YouTube video. Video timestamps are approximate.
  • Use the Chrome extension on any page: pick the module and save. Sign in on the website first, and the extension uses that session.
  • Scanned PDFs without selectable text cannot be read, and you are told so straight away.
3Build and explore the concept web
  1. In the Materials panel, select Build concept web. It reads your sources and finds concepts and their links.
  2. On the Study map, switch between Concept web, Material map and the keyboard Outline.
  3. Select a concept for its definition and source. Select a link label to read the quote from each side.
  4. Use the zoom buttons at the bottom right, or scroll the canvas.
4Practise with a quiz or a boss battle
  1. Choose Practice quiz, then Start quiz. Weakest concepts come first.
  2. Pick an answer. You see right away whether it was correct, plus the citation chip that opens the source page.
  3. For a game, choose Boss battle: three hearts, XP for each correct answer, and missed concepts come back until you win.
5Find your learning gap
  1. After a wrong answer, select Show learning gap.
  2. The map lights the path from the question's concept back to the likely gap, with the quote that explains it.
  3. Select See revision path for the order to review: the gap first, then a few easy questions on it, then the concepts in between, then a retry.
6Plan your revision
  1. Open Revision plan and choose how many minutes a day you can study.
  2. Concepts are ordered by exam weight times how far you are from ready. Select one to open it.
  3. The plan updates as you practise.
7Fix lecture captions
  1. Open Lecture captions and upload a lecture recording with its slides.
  2. Highlighted words are likely mishearings of terms from your slides. Select the suggestion to fix it, or edit the words yourself.
  3. Select a concept to jump the recording to where it is explained.
8Ask your materials
  1. Select Ask materials in the module header and type a question.
  2. The answer cites the pages it used. If your material does not cover the question, you get "not in your materials".
9Make it comfortable to read

Open Accessibility at the bottom of the sidebar to turn on the readable font, high contrast or reduce motion. Everything works by keyboard: Tab to move, Enter to open, arrow keys in the outline, and Esc to close a dialog.
